# Article Patterns

Use this reference when planning the article type, outline, title, opening, ending, or mobile rhythm.

## Article Types

Insight or opinion:
- Use when the user wants a point of view, industry observation, or trend interpretation.
- Structure: scene or conflict, core judgment, evidence, implications, counterpoint, takeaway.
- Keep the claim explicit. A weak opinion article usually hides behind summary.

Explainer:
- Use when the reader needs to understand a concept, policy, product, method, or event.
- Structure: why it matters, what it is, how it works, what changes for the reader, limits, next step.
- Use analogies sparingly and check that they do not distort the concept.

Practical guide:
- Use when the user wants "how to", workflow, checklist, tutorial, or operational advice.
- Structure: target scenario, prerequisites, steps, examples, common mistakes, checklist.
- Keep steps actionable. Avoid advice that cannot be executed.

Product or tool review:
- Use when evaluating a tool, service, AI product, app, or workflow.
- Structure: use case, who it fits, core experience, strengths, tradeoffs, comparison, recommendation.
- Anchor claims in actual tasks instead of listing features.

Case study:
- Use when explaining a project, campaign, organization, customer story, or internal practice.
- Structure: background, challenge, decision, execution, result, lessons.
- Include constraints and failed attempts if available; they make the story credible.

Event recap:
- Use for forums, launches, salons, training, or community events.
- Structure: event context, highlight moments, key ideas, participant value, next action.
- Avoid chronological minutes unless the user explicitly asks for them.

Brand or announcement article:
- Use when introducing a product, team, program, partnership, or milestone.
- Structure: what happened, why it matters, proof, user benefit, next step.
- Keep promotional language restrained and concrete.

Interview or profile:
- Use when turning a conversation, transcript, or bio into a readable article.
- Structure: person or theme hook, defining tension, selected stories, insights, closing quote or reflection.
- Preserve voice without dumping the transcript.

Conversion article:
- Use when the goal is sign-up, consultation, purchase, event registration, or community joining.
- Structure: reader problem, cost of inaction, solution, proof, offer, CTA.
- Make the CTA direct but not noisy.

## Mobile-First Rhythm

- Keep most paragraphs between 1 and 4 Chinese lines.
- Use section headings as navigation, not decoration.
- Alternate explanation with examples, lists, contrast, or questions.
- Put complex background after the reader already knows why it matters.
- Avoid stacking multiple long lists; compress or turn them into steps.

## Title Patterns

Use title candidates that match the article's actual substance:

- Question: `为什么越来越多团队开始重写自己的知识库？`
- Contrast: `不是缺工具，而是缺一套能跑起来的流程`
- Scene: `我用一周时间，把活动复盘从 3 小时压到 30 分钟`
- Promise: `一篇讲清楚：小团队如何做公众号选题`
- Judgment: `这类 AI 工具真正有价值的地方，不在聊天`
- Number: `5 个检查点，让公众号推文少一点空话`

Avoid titles that overpromise, rely only on "震撼/爆火/必看", or imply facts not supported by the article.

## Opening Patterns

Concrete scene:
- Start with a recognizable moment, friction, or decision.
- Best for practical, product, case, and conversion articles.

Sharp question:
- Ask the question the article will actually answer.
- Best for opinion, explainer, and trend articles.

Contradiction:
- Present a common belief, then show what is wrong or incomplete.
- Best for thought pieces and reviews.

Result first:
- Lead with an outcome, then explain the process.
- Best for case studies, event recaps, and tutorials.

## Ending Patterns

- Takeaway: summarize the one judgment the reader should remember.
- Checklist: turn the article into action.
- Question: invite comments when community engagement is the goal.
- CTA: direct readers to register, consult, follow, reply, or download.
- Reflection: close a narrative or interview with a human note.

Do not add a CTA that conflicts with the article's tone or evidence.
